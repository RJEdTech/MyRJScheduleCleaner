Why this happens

Your MyRJ feed contains two kinds of events: your timed class blocks, and all-day markers like “White Day” and “Red Day — One-Hour Late Start.”

The feed never states whether those all-day markers should count as busy time. When a calendar file leaves that out, the iCalendar standard says to assume busy — so Outlook marks you unavailable from midnight to midnight on every school day. Colleagues checking your availability see a solid wall, and Scheduling Assistant stops being useful to anyone trying to book you.

This page adds the one missing line to those all-day events so they still display but no longer block your time. Nothing else is touched — not your class blocks, not the timezone rules, not the event IDs.

Your data never leaves this computer. The file is processed in your browser. Nothing is uploaded, stored, or transmitted — you can disconnect from the internet and this page still works.

Not “School Calendars”
Further down that page is a separate School Calendars section. Those are the whole school's calendars — roughly 1,700 events, nearly all of them other people's games, practices, and meetings. Importing one buries your own schedule and marks you busy for things you aren't attending. Stay in the My Calendars section.
Right-click, don't left-click
Left-clicking hands the link to Windows, which asks which app should open it and then passes a broken version to Outlook — that's the “Invalid URL” error.

Read this before you rely on it

This is a snapshot, not a subscription — so keep your subscribed calendar too.

These events are now yours. They block your time and reach your phone. But they are frozen as of today. When the Academic Office changes something in MyRJ — a special schedule, or a one-hour late start called at 5:30am for weather — that change will never reach this imported copy. On a delay morning it won't just be out of date; it will be showing block times that are no longer real.

Subscribe to the same feed as well (Add calendar → Subscribe from web) and keep it visible. The import is what makes you look busy to colleagues; the subscription is what tells you the truth. When they disagree, the subscribed one is right.

Run this again each semester
Delete the previous imported set first. Outlook does not reliably match re-imported events to the ones already there, so importing over the top leaves you with two of everything.
